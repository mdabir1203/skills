# Platform Launch Algorithms (Product Hunt, Hacker News, App Store, Indie Hackers — 2026)

Each platform is an algorithm. Decode the algorithm first, then exploit it. The mistake 2024 playbooks make: treating Product Hunt and Hacker News and Indie Hackers as "launch venues" with similar tactics. They are not similar. They are four different algorithms with four different rules, and the wrong play on each one wastes the launch.

This is **Channel 3** in the distribution stack. The most-overrated channel. The 2024 advice to "launch everywhere at once" is wrong. Pick **one** platform, run it correctly, and you get 80% of the value. Launch on all four, you get 20% of each.

## The "pick one" rule

You cannot launch on Product Hunt AND Hacker News AND Indie Hackers on the same week. The math does not work.

- Each launch requires 2–6 weeks of prep, all of it serial.
- The energy required to handle comments and DMs on day 0 is full-time. You cannot do that on two platforms simultaneously.
- Splitting your mailing list, your network, and your launch allies across platforms dilutes each one.

**The right move:** pick the platform that matches your product type, run it once, and never run it again until the next major version.

## The 4 platforms, decoded

### Product Hunt — the badge and SEO play

**The algorithm:** weighted first-3-hour engagement. Each upvote in the first hour counts more than upvotes later. Comments are weighted ~3x upvotes. The leaderboard recalculates every 30 minutes. Top 5 of the day is the goal. Top 3 unlocks newsletter features. Outside top 10, the value is the badge, not the traffic.

**When to use it:**
- B2B SaaS or prosumer tools
- You have a network of 200+ people who will upvote and comment in the first 3 hours
- You have a polished product (the gallery images, video, and tagline are the difference between top 10 and top 5)

**When NOT to use it:**
- Week 0 of launch. You need testimonials first.
- Pure dev tools (HN will reward you more).
- Consumer apps with no network of B2B supporters.
- Without a 4-week prep window.

**The week-by-week prep (4–6 weeks out):**

- **Week –6:** pick the launch date. Tuesday or Wednesday. Never Monday or Friday. 12:01 AM PT submission.
- **Week –5:** book a hunter. A hunter with a following is the single biggest boost. Reach out 4+ weeks ahead.
- **Week –4:** produce assets. 60–90 second demo video. 4 gallery images (hero, 3 features or testimonials). Tagline <60 chars, outcome-driven.
- **Week –3:** prep the maker's first comment. This is the "discussion starter" that shows up in the thread. Plan it as a Q&A with yourself: what it does, who it is for, what's next.
- **Week –2:** warm up. Comment on 10 other PH launches genuinely. Build relationships with the top hunters in your category.
- **Week –1:** brief your supporters. Email 50–100 people with a specific ask: "upvote and one specific comment in the first 3 hours." Make the ask concrete.
- **Day 0:** post the maker's first comment within 2 minutes of submission. Respond to every comment within 10 minutes for the first 3 hours. Every. Single. One.
- **Day +1:** thank-you email to supporters. PH badge screenshot for the landing page.

**Realistic outcomes:**
- Top 5 of the day: 200–500 visitors, 15–40 signups, dofollow backlink, badge for landing page.
- Top 3 of the day: above + newsletter features (3–5 industry newsletters), 500–2,000 visitors.
- Outside top 10: 50–100 visitors. The badge is the value.

**The cut:** if you do not hit top 10, the launch wasted your network's patience. Do not relaunch for 6 months. The dofollow backlink from PH is the long-tail win, not the day-of traffic.

### Hacker News — the technical depth play

**The algorithm:** upvote velocity in the first 60 minutes, weighted by submitter karma and comment quality. HN is merciless about hype. The community detects "marketing voice" in 5 words and downvotes it into the ground. Front page = 10,000+ visits. 1–3% conversion (but very high-quality users).

**When to use it:**
- Technical products (developer tools, infra, dev frameworks, AI infra, anything with code)
- You have a genuinely interesting technical story (not "I used React," but "I built a custom regex engine to solve X")
- You can write in HN's voice (terse, technical, no marketing words)

**When NOT to use it:**
- B2B SaaS for non-technical buyers. The HN audience is not your buyer.
- Products without a technical angle.
- Without a "Show HN" format that passes the sniff test.

**The "Show HN" format:**

- Title: `Show HN: [Product] – [one-line technical description]`
- NOT "Show HN: We raised $2M for..." — they will bury you.
- NOT "Show HN: The future of [category]" — too vague.

The post body:
- Self-post, not a link. HN deprioritizes link posts.
- First paragraph: what it is, who built it, why.
- Second paragraph: how it works, technical details.
- Third paragraph: why you built it, what problem.
- Link at the bottom, optional.
- Length: 200–400 words. Shorter is better. HN is not Medium.

**The timing:** weekday morning, US time. 6–10 AM ET is the sweet spot. The HN crowd reads the morning batch with coffee.

**The comments section is the actual launch.** Spend 4 hours in the comments. Answer every technical question with depth. Acknowledge criticism. "You're right, that approach has problems, here's what I tried instead" wins. "Actually, you're wrong" loses. The founder who engages thoughtfully in the comments can carry a mediocre post. The founder who disappears loses even a great post.

**Realistic outcomes:**
- Front page (top 30): 5,000–30,000 visits in 24 hours, 50–500 signups, lots of GitHub stars if open source.
- Top 5 of Show HN: 10,000–50,000 visits, hundreds of signups.
- Off the front page in 4 hours: 500–2,000 visits. Still worth it for the long-tail comment thread (it ranks in Google and is cited by LLMs).

**The cut:** if your post is off the front page in 4 hours, do not resubmit. The HN community remembers. One bad Show HN is forgivable. Two is a pattern.

### App Store editorial — the only mobile launch

**The algorithm:** App Store editorial is the only launch that matters for mobile apps. The "featured" tab gets 30x the traffic of the top of the search results. Editorial features are awarded by a small team at Apple based on: design quality, originality, localization, and downloads velocity in the first 72 hours.

**When to use it:**
- Mobile app launch (iOS, primarily).
- You have a polished design. Apple rewards design quality above all else.
- You have 4+ localizations. The App Store editorial team reviews apps in every locale; apps with only English are filtered out of non-English features.

**When NOT to use it:**
- Web app. Wrong platform.
- First version. The first version rarely gets featured. The v1.1 or v1.2 with real traction is more likely.
- Without ASO basics (title, subtitle, keyword field, screenshots, description).

**The prep (4–6 weeks out):**

- **Week –6:** App Store Optimization (ASO) basics. Title (30 chars, keyword-loaded). Subtitle (30 chars, value proposition). Keyword field (100 chars, comma-separated). Screenshots (5–8, with text overlays explaining the value). Description (first 3 lines matter — they are visible without "more").
- **Week –5:** pitch Apple editorial. Form at `developer.apple.com/contact/app-store/promote/`. Be specific: which app, which feature, which audience, which countries.
- **Week –4:** submit for review. Have the app in TestFlight, ready to ship.
- **Week –3:** prep launch day assets. Press kit, demo video, screenshots in landscape, app icon variants.
- **Week –2:** brief your network for the rating boost. 50+ ratings in the first 72 hours with 4.5+ stars matters for the editorial team's review.
- **Day 0:** submit the app. Monitor crash rate and reviews. Respond to every 1-star review within 24 hours.
- **Day +30:** request the editorial feature via the form again if not already featured.

**Realistic outcomes:**
- Featured by Apple: 50,000–500,000 downloads in the first week. App of the Day is the top tier.
- Top 100 in category: 5,000–20,000 downloads in the first week.
- Outside top 100: 500–2,000 downloads. Most apps land here. ASO iteration is the path up.

**The cut:** if the app does not break top 100 in the first week, the bottleneck is ASO or screenshots, not the launch. Iterate. Re-submit screenshots with different text overlays every 2 weeks until one converts.

### Indie Hackers — the transparent founder play

**The algorithm:** weekday morning US time (9–11 AM ET). Format-driven. Transparency rewarded (revenue numbers, tech stack, distribution plan). Self-Promote Saturday is a feature, not a fallback. Top threads get 100–200 visitors, 8–15 signups.

**When to use it:**
- Solo founder or small team.
- You have revenue or traction to share (even $47 MRR counts).
- You are willing to be transparent about your stack, your numbers, and your failures.

**When NOT to use it:**
- Funded startup with no public metrics. IH is for bootstrappers.
- Without a real number to share. "0 users but I am working hard" is the most-downvoted post on IH.
- Without engaging in other threads first. Pure self-promotion is flagged.

**The "Show IH" format:**

- Title: `Show IH: [Product] – [revenue/traction in numbers]`
- Body: tech stack, distribution plan, MRR, what is working, what is not. Specific numbers. Specific tools. Specific lessons.
- The thread that works: "I made $1,200 MRR in 90 days. Here's exactly how." Specific wins AND specific losses.

**The cut:** if your Show IH post gets <50 upvotes, the format is wrong. Either you are not transparent enough, you are not specific enough, or you are posting at the wrong time. Iterate the format before relaunching.

## The cross-platform launch sequence

If you are launching v1.0 and want to hit multiple platforms, do them in series, not in parallel. Each one requires full focus.

- **Week 0:** Product Hunt launch. (Tuesday or Wednesday.)
- **Week 2:** Hacker News Show HN. (Weekday morning.)
- **Week 4:** Indie Hackers Show IH. (Weekday morning US time.)
- **Week 6+:** App Store editorial pitch (if mobile). This takes 4+ weeks for Apple to respond.

Run community (Channel 2) and LLM citation (Channel 1) in parallel throughout. SEO (Channel 4) is a 6-month compounding play, not a launch event.

## The 6 anti-patterns

1. **Launching everywhere at once.** Dilutes every platform. Pick one.
2. **Launching without a network.** PH without 200 supporters is a wasted launch. Build the list first.
3. **Launching on a Friday or weekend.** Lowest traffic window on every platform.
4. **"We just launched, would love feedback."** Empty. No number. No story. Auto-downvoted.
5. **Asking for upvotes explicitly.** Banned on PH, downvoted on HN, ignored on IH.
6. **Re-launching after a failed launch.** The platforms remember. Wait 6 months and bring a new story.

## The metric that proves this channel works

Track:
- **Signups in the 7 days after launch** (with UTM `?utm_source=producthunt` / `?utm_source=hackernews` / etc.)
- **Long-tail citation count** (the launch thread ranks in Google and is cited by LLMs for months)
- **Backlinks earned** (the dofollow PH backlink is the durable SEO win)

If a launch produces <10 signups and 0 backlinks, the launch did not work. Do not relaunch. Iterate the product, the positioning, or the assets, and try again in 6 months.

## How to apply this reference

When this reference is loaded, produce:

1. Which platform to launch on, based on product type (with reasoning).
2. The 4–6 week prep plan, week by week, with specific tasks.
3. The day-of runbook, hour by hour, with the specific ask for supporters.
4. The realistic outcome range (top 5, top 10, off-front-page).
5. The cut threshold: when to abandon a launch, when to relaunch.
6. The cross-platform sequence if multiple platforms are in scope.

Do not produce generic "post on Product Hunt" advice. Every recommendation must be platform-specific: algorithm factor, prep week, day-of hour, format template.
