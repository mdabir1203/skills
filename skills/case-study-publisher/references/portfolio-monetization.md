# Monetizing your portfolio

Your portfolio site is the storefront. Medium, Dev.to, Hashnode, HackerNoon — those are the billboards that drive traffic to the storefront. This playbook covers how to turn that traffic into actual money.

## The honest baseline (2026, mid-level engineer)

A solo engineering portfolio site makes money through five channels, ranked by realistic revenue:

| Channel | Realistic monthly revenue (USD) | Realistic annual | Effort |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Direct consulting / fractional CTO** | $2,000-15,000 | $24k-180k | High (sales + delivery) |
| **Recruiter inbound (full-time roles)** | $0 direct, but $20k-50k/yr in salary uplift | $20k-50k salary delta | Medium (portfolio + presence) |
| **Digital products (templates, courses, ebooks)** | $200-3,000 | $2.4k-36k | High (build once, sell many) |
| **Affiliate / referral (hosting, platforms)** | $50-500 | $600-6k | Low (link insertion) |
| **Sponsorships / sponsored posts** | $500-3,000 per post | $6k-36k | Medium (needs audience) |

**The single biggest lever is consulting/fractional CTO work.** Every other channel feeds the top of the funnel. Case studies prove you can ship. Portfolio projects prove you can architect. Together they're a sales letter that runs 24/7.

## Portfolio structure (what converts)

A portfolio site that turns visitors into leads has 5 sections, in this order:

### 1. Hero (above the fold, 3 seconds to "I want to talk to this person")

- Name, role, one-line positioning
- One credibility signal: years of experience, named clients, or a metric
- One CTA: "Book a 30-min intro call" (Calendly or Cal.com link)
- Optional: a 30-second video of you talking directly to the camera. Conversion rate: 2-3x static text.

### 2. Featured case studies (3-5 max, deep links)

Each one is a 200-word summary with: the problem, what you built, the result (with a number), a link to the full Medium case study.

**Direct content from your Medium case studies is the highest-converting portfolio content you have.** This is why the cross-post loop matters: Medium builds authority, the portfolio converts it.

### 3. Technical skills (visual, scannable, not a CV dump)

Group by category:
- Backend (Node.js, Express, Cloudflare Workers, D1, R2)
- Frontend (Electron, React, TypeScript, vanilla JS)
- Infrastructure (Cloudflare Tunnel, PM2, NSIS packaging, electron-updater)
- Domains (factory IoT, distributed systems, data integrity)

Don't list every tool you've ever touched. List the ones you'd hire yourself for.

### 4. About / credentials (2 paragraphs max)

- 1 paragraph: who you are, where you are, what you do
- 1 paragraph: the credibility signals (degrees, prior work, GitHub stars, conference talks)
- Photo (real, not stock). People hire people, not logos.

### 5. Contact / booking

- Calendly or Cal.com link (single CTA)
- Email (with a note: "I read every email. I reply within 24 hours on weekdays.")
- LinkedIn, GitHub, Medium (secondary)
- DO NOT: phone number (Doxxing risk if public), home address, or "DM me on Twitter" alone

## Monetization mechanics

### 1. Direct consulting / fractional CTO

**How to position:**
- "Fractional CTO for [your niche] startups"
- "Senior engineer for hire: 1-3 month engagements, $X/day"

**How to price (mid-level market, 2026):**
- Day rate: $1,000-2,500 for senior freelance
- Fractional CTO retainer: $6,000-15,000/month for 1-2 days/week
- Project-based: quote by the deliverable, not the hour

**How to close:**
- The Calendly link is the gate. Free 30-min intro call. 50% of intro calls convert to paid work if your portfolio is sharp.
- Have a 1-page engagement scope template ready. "Here's how a typical engagement works." Removes friction.

**Required for this to work:**
- 3+ case studies on your portfolio (the cross-post loop feeds this)
- A clear "who I work with" statement
- Testimonials from past clients

### 2. Recruiter inbound (full-time roles)

**How to position:**
- Public LinkedIn profile with "Open to [level] [role] roles in [location] or remote"
- Portfolio site with a "Resume" page (downloadable PDF, ATS-friendly)

**The play:**
- Recruiters Google for "[your stack] developer [your city]" or "[specialty] engineer"
- Your case studies rank for those searches
- They land on your portfolio, see your GitHub, see your contact info
- They reach out

**Conversion rate:** expect 2-5 inbound recruiter messages per month if your case studies are indexed and you have 10+ posts on Medium/Dev.to.

### 3. Digital products (templates, courses, ebooks)

**What sells in your niche:**
- A production-ready template for your stack — $49-149
- An ebook or guide on a specific problem you've solved — $19-49
- A course on a non-obvious skill — $99-299

**Where to sell:**
- Gumroad (zero setup, 10% fee) — easiest
- Lemon Squeezy (handles VAT/tax globally) — better for international sales
- Your own site with Stripe — most control, most setup

**Realistic:** $200-3,000/month once you have 2-3 products and an email list.

**The compounding play:** every Medium case study can become a paid product. The case study is the marketing; the product is the conversion.

### 4. Affiliate / referral

**Programs that fit most engineering stacks:**
- Cloudflare (referral program)
- Vercel (referral program)
- DigitalOcean (referral program)
- GitHub (no direct program, but "sponsor me" button on repos)

**Honest take:** affiliate income is small. Treat it as bonus, not core. Don't compromise content for affiliate placement.

### 5. Sponsorships / sponsored posts

**When you have 5,000+ Medium followers, sponsorships open up.** Most platforms have a "sponsored" or "boost" mechanism.

For engineering content, sponsors will be B2B SaaS companies:
- Monitoring tools (Sentry, LogRocket, Highlight.io)
- Cloud platforms (Cloudflare itself, Vercel, Railway)
- Developer tools (Linear, Notion, Retool)

**Realistic pricing:** $500-3,000 per sponsored post when you have 5k+ Medium followers and 1k+ Dev.to followers. Lower with smaller audiences.

## Traffic loop (Medium → Portfolio → Revenue)

```
[ Medium case study ]
        ↓ (one CTA at the end)
[ Your portfolio site ]
        ↓
[ Calendly / Email / LinkedIn ]
        ↓
[ 30-min intro call ]
        ↓
[ Paid engagement ]
```

Every Medium post ends with a single, soft CTA:
> "I'm [Your Name]. I [your one-line]. More of my work: {{PORTFOLIO_URL}}"

That's it. No popups. No email capture modals. One link, one line.

## What to do this week (concrete actions)

1. **Audit the current portfolio site.** Is it on the structure above? If not, fix the top 3 issues first (hero, case studies, contact).
2. **Write the next Medium case study** (use the skill — it does this for you).
3. **Set up a Calendly** (free tier works) and link it from the portfolio.
4. **Ask a past client/colleague for a 3-sentence testimonial.** Real names, real metrics.
5. **Post 1 LinkedIn update per case study** with the Medium link. This drives the most recruiter traffic per minute of effort.

## What NOT to do

- Don't add a paywall to your case studies. The case studies are the marketing. Gate them and you lose the funnel.
- Don't build a course before you have an audience. The audience has to come first.
- Don't chase sponsorships before you have 5,000 Medium followers. The math doesn't work below that.
- Don't run ads to your portfolio. The CAC for solo engineers running personal ads is brutal.

## The 12-month compounding play

| Month | Milestone | Expected monthly revenue |
|---|---|---|
| 0 (now) | Portfolio audit, Calendly, first case study published | $0 |
| 1 | 3 case studies on Medium, portfolio has 3 deep case studies | $0 |
| 2 | First recruiter inbound, first consulting inquiry | $0-2,000 |
| 3 | First paid consulting engagement | $2,000-8,000 |
| 6 | 8+ case studies, 1 digital product launched, 2-3 retainer clients | $4,000-15,000 |
| 12 | 15+ case studies, 3 digital products, 5+ retainer clients, sponsorships open | $8,000-30,000 |

The compounding asset is the case-study library. Every post makes the next one easier to write, ranks higher in search, and converts better. The portfolio site is the storefront; the case studies are the inventory. Build the inventory first.
